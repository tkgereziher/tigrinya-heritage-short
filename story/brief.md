# Story brief (input to `python film.py draft`)

- **Narration language:** Tigrinya (ti), Ge'ez script. Fallback: Amharic (am).
- **Source:** a traditional Tigrinya animal fable (ዛንታ) told in our family. Folk tale, no real people.
- **Title:** ንጉስ ኣራዊት ኣያ ኣንበሳ (እቲ ሳዕሪ ቀጠልያ ድዩ ሰማያዊ?): "King of the Beasts, Aya Anbesa (Is the grass green or blue?)"

## The story in 4 sentences
In a great forest where all animals live in peace, Aya Anbesa the lion rules fairly and is respected by all.
A donkey insists the grass is blue; the leopard says it is green, and their argument turns into a quarrel, so
they go to the king for judgement. The lion listens and calmly rules: "Yes, the grass is blue." The donkey
leaves laughing, and when the hurt leopard asks why, the lion smiles: "I know the grass is green. Your
punishment is not about the grass. It is because you, with all your wisdom, wasted your time arguing with a
fool who will not understand, and brought it to me."

## Where and when
A great green forest-savanna in the highlands: acacia trees, tall green grass, a rocky outcrop that serves as
the lion's "palace" (ቤተ-መንግስቲ). Daylight, warm afternoon light, golden hour for the final lesson.

## Characters (animals, stylised; keep them identical in every shot)
- **aya_anbesa** (ኣያ ኣንበሳ, King Lion): an old, calm, dignified lion with a thick dark-golden mane, a few
  grey hairs on the muzzle, kind amber eyes, a simple woven gold band (crown) on his head.
- **adgi** (ኣድጊ, the donkey): a stubborn grey donkey with a white muzzle and belly, very long ears with dark
  tips, a short bristly mane, a small red tassel on his halter.
- **nebri** (ነብሪ, the leopard): a slender, clever leopard with a golden coat and black rosettes, bright green eyes, long tail.

## Audience (PROPOSED, confirm or change)
Tigrinya-speaking families, especially parents in the diaspora watching with children aged 6–12, and young
adults who grew up hearing these tales. They watch on a phone, vertical, shared on WhatsApp/TikTok/Facebook.
Many children understand some Tigrinya but read English, hence the English subtitles.

## Intent (PROPOSED)
They laugh at the lion's surprising verdict, then get the twist: **don't waste your wisdom and time arguing
with someone who refuses to see.** Parents should want to retell the tale at home.

## Look
Warm hand-painted storybook illustration, gouache texture, soft grain, rich greens and ochres, gentle light.
**The grass must clearly be GREEN in every shot**: the joke depends on it.

## Narration
One warm storyteller voice, like an elder telling a tale to children: unhurried, playful in the quarrel, calm
and wise for the lion. Open with the traditional «ነበረያ ነበረ!».

## Music
Instrumental, light and playful Ethiopian krar plucking with soft masenqo and gentle hand percussion. It
builds slightly during the quarrel, goes quiet for the verdict, and returns warm for the lion's lesson. No vocals.
